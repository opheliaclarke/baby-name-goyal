The shortlist

Three names that clear every gate

To survive, a name had to do four things at once: start with one of your Pandit's syllables, carry a real meaning attested in Monier-Williams, land on a fortunate Cheiro compound, and reduce to a root that is friendly to both Moolank 1 and Bhagyank 9. Out of 150-plus candidates, these came out on top.

Preference 1
Dhyann
ध्यान
DHYAAN · two syllables
D4 H5 Y1 A1 N5 N5= 21 → 3 · Jupiter

Why this one first

It lands on the single best number available to him. 21 reduces to 3 — Jupiter, and Jupiter is the one root that is a friend of the Sun and a friend of Mars, so it satisfies both his numbers at once. The chart says the same thing independently: Jupiter rules Sagittarius, the sign his Moon is in, and sits exalted in his chart. The numerology and the astrology point at the same planet without being made to.

What it means

Meditation. Sustained, undivided attention. ध्यान is the seventh limb of Patanjali's eightfold yoga — the stage where concentration deepens into absorption. The word travelled out of Sanskrit into Chinese as Chan and into Japanese as Zen.

It is clean all the way down

Every candidate was checked against Monier-Williams and Apte for buried second meanings, community collisions and English accidents. Most names pick up something. Dhyan came back with nothing against it — no negative sense, no caste marking, no awkward English reading, unambiguously male.

How it ages

Two syllables, no consonant cluster, no silent letters. A teacher in Delhi, London or Singapore reads it right the first time. It is current in Indian birth registers now and carries no 1950s echo.

The one thing to weigh

Spelt plainly, Dhyan totals 16 — the Tower struck by lightning, one of Cheiro's worst. The second n is doing real work and is not decoration. If your family or your Pandit will not accept an added letter, take Dhanvin below instead — but do not use the short spelling.

Preference 2
Dhanvin
धन्विन्
DHAN-vin
D4 H5 A1 N5 V6 I1 N5= 27 → 9 · Mars

Why this one

It is the only name on the entire shortlist that needed no help at all — no doubled letter, no added vowel, no numerological respelling. Written exactly the way Sanskrit already writes it, it lands on 27. If an untouched spelling matters to you, this is your name and it should be first.

What it means

Armed with a bow. The bowman. From धन्वन् dhanvan, a bow. Attested as an epithet of Shiva (Shiva Purana 2.2.41.31, Linga Purana 95.47), of Vishnu, and of Arjuna — the three great archers.

The alignment nobody planned

His Moon sits in Sagittarius — धनु, Dhanu, Sanskrit for the bow. The sign he was born under and the name mean the same thing. That was not engineered; it fell out of the letter Dh, which itself came from the Moon sitting in that very sign.

The numbers

27 is Cheiro's Sceptre — authority, command, the reward of the intellect. It reduces to 9, Mars, which is his own Bhagyank, so the name and the destiny number are the same planet. With the surname it becomes 42, also fortunate.

The one thing to weigh

Two disclosures. Monier-Williams gives धन्वन् a co-equal second sense, “desert, waste land”, and the adjective can also gloss cunning, shrewd. Neither is the sense used in the scriptural passages above, but a Sanskrit-literate relative can open a dictionary and find them, so you should hear it here first. It is also uncommon enough that he will spell it out sometimes.

Preference 3
Dharmik
धार्मिक
DHAR-mik
D4 H5 A1 R2 M4 I1 K2= 19 → 1 · Sun

Why this one

It reaches 19, which Cheiro rates the most fortunate compound in the whole system — he calls it the Sun, the Prince of Heaven, a number of success, honour and happiness. It gets there on its ordinary spelling, and 19 reduces to 1, the Sun, which is his own Moolank.

What it means

Righteous, virtuous, just. From धर्म, the idea of right order and duty — the most load-bearing word in the tradition. It appears in the Ganesha Sahasranama as name 187.

Why it is safe where its cousins are not

This matters: Dharmesh, Dharmendra and Dharmaraj are all classical epithets of Yama, the god of death — unanimous across five lexicons. Most families do not know that. Dharmik is built differently and carries none of it.

The one thing to weigh

The strict classical form is Dhārmika, with a long ā; Dharmik is the Hindi clip of it, which is what everyone actually uses. And with the surname it totals 34, which Cheiro grades mixed rather than fortunate. You chose to judge on the first name alone, so that does not disqualify it — but Dhyann and Dhanvin are clean on both readings, which is why they rank above it.

Sound — your Pandit's set, one quarter off

A legitimate Purva Ashadha syllable, belonging to pada 1 and, in some tables, pada 3. Good names exist and several clear the numbers cleanly. It is simply not the quarter your son was actually born in, so it ranks second.

F फ
Drop it — the letter has nothing to give

Not a matter of taste. The entire फ section of Monier-Williams runs to 521 headwords that collapse onto about eight roots, and roughly 250 of them are abstract compounds of phala, fruit. Of the handful usable as names, the Phani family is destroyed in English by an obscene homophone, and the Phalgun family sits on a root that Monier-Williams and Apte both gloss as pithless, worthless, feeble, untrue. Not one F name clears both the meaning screen and the numbers.

The arithmetic

Why only seven totals were allowed

Indian name numerology asks two separate questions of a name, and most shortlists only ask the first. A name has to answer both.

Moolank · the birth number

1Sun
from the day alone
28 → 2 + 8 = 10 → 1 + 0 = 1

28 July also falls inside what Cheiro calls the House of the Sun (21 Jul – 28 Aug), which strengthens a number-1 birth.

Bhagyank · the destiny number

9Mars
from the whole date
2+8+0+7+2+0+2+6 = 27 → 2 + 7 = 9

Gate 1 · the compound must be fortunate

Cheiro grades every compound number from 10 to 52. A name totalling 23 or 27 is blessed; a name totalling 16, 26 or 29 carries an explicit warning no matter how beautiful it sounds.

Gate 2 · the root must befriend both 1 and 9

The total is then reduced to a single digit, which maps to a planet. That planet has to be a friend of the Sun and a friend of Mars. This is the gate almost every popular name fails — and it is why Dhruv does not work here: it totals 23, Cheiro's most fortunate compound of all, but 23 reduces to 5 = Mercury, and Mercury is the declared enemy of Mars. A perfect compound sitting on a hostile root.

Seven totals survive both gates. Every name on this page lands on one of them.

One thing worth knowing about the method

You asked me to judge the first name on its own. That is a legitimate reading, but most Indian practitioners score the full name, and Cheiro himself scored the name a person is actually called by. So the two schools can disagree. They do not disagree here: Dhyann becomes 36 with the surname and Dhanvin becomes 42, and both of those are fortunate too. The top two answers hold whichever way you score them.
