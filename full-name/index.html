Part two · with the surname attached

Goyal weighs 15. That moves the whole board.

Scoring “Firstname Goyal” instead of the first name alone is not a small adjustment. The surname adds a fixed 15 to every candidate, which shifts the entire range upward and opens a completely different set of windows. Names that were perfect on their own stop working. Names that failed on their own start working. One name survives both readings.

The headline

Only one first-name total survives both readings: 21.

Every other total that passes on the first name alone fails once Goyal is added, and every total that works with Goyal fails on its own — except 21, which lands on 36 with the surname. Both are fortunate, and both reduce to roots friendly to his numbers. Dhyann is 21. That is the answer that does not depend on which school you follow.

The names that clear the full-name gate

Same two tests as before, applied to “Firstname Goyal” as one string: the total must be a fortunate Cheiro compound, and it must reduce to a root friendly to both Moolank 1 and Bhagyank 9. The letter is still Dh, Bh or F, from pada 4 of Purva Ashadha.

Why this survives and nothing else does

21 is the only first-name total that satisfies both readings. On its own it is the Crown of the Magi; with Goyal attached it becomes 36, which Cheiro files with 27, the Sceptre. Root 9 — Mars — which is his own Bhagyank and a friend of his Moolank. You do not have to decide which school is right, because this name answers both.

What it means

Meditation. Sustained, undivided attention. ध्यान is the seventh limb of Patanjali's eightfold yoga. The word travelled out of Sanskrit into Chinese as Chan and into Japanese as Zen.

How the pair sounds

Dhyann Goyal is two syllables against two — even weight, no collision at the join, and no repeated consonant to trip over. It reads cleanly on a passport and in a classroom register.

The one thing to weigh

Unchanged from before: plain Dhyan totals 16, one of Cheiro's worst, and 16 + 15 gives 31 which is also poor. The second n carries both readings, not just one. Under the full-name test it matters even more than it did.

Why this one

Also 21, so it clears both gates exactly as Dhyann does — 36 with the surname, root 9, Mars. If you want a second name that carries no compromise under either school, this is it.

What it means

Steadfast, composed, resolute, wise. धीर is the settled quality of someone who does not panic — used in the epics for the self-possessed rather than merely the brave.

Short, and global without trying

One syllable, five letters, no cluster. It is about as portable as an Indian name gets, and it keeps its meaning intact abroad rather than being flattened into a nickname.

The one thing to weigh

Spelling is load-bearing here in two separate ways. Numerically, Dhir and Dheer are the same Sanskrit word but Dhir totals 12 and fails on its own — it only works once Goyal is attached. Separately, Dhir also exists as a North Indian surname, so “Dhir Goyal” can read as two surnames stacked. Both problems disappear with the Dheer spelling. Write it Dheer.

The surprise on this page

I told you the F letter was a dead end, and under the first-name reading it is — Phalit totals 22, which Cheiro calls the good man blinded. With Goyal attached it becomes 37, a fortunate compound reducing to 1, the Sun, which is his own Moolank. The surname rescues the letter your Pandit gave you third.

What it means

Fruitful. Successful. Fulfilled — specifically of a wish or an effort that has borne its result. From √फल्, to bear fruit. Cleanly attested in both Monier-Williams and Apte.

It is not the bad F root

This matters, because the other F names fail on their root. Phalit comes from फल phala, fruit. The names I rejected — Phalgun, Phalguna — come from फल्गु phalgu, a different word glossed pithless, worthless, feeble, untrue. Same first syllable in English, unrelated in Sanskrit.

The one thing to weigh

It is rare to the point of being almost unused as a given name, which cuts both ways: nothing to live down, but also nothing familiar to lean on. And it works only with the surname attached — on its own it is a poor number, so this name commits you to the full-name reading.

The Venus thread

Bhargava is a name of Shukra — Venus. Venus rules Purva Ashadha, the nakshatra his Moon sat in, and Venus governs his birth mahadasha. The surname Goyal reduces to 6, which is also Venus. This name leans into the Venus signature rather than away from it, which is a deliberate choice rather than an accident.

What it means

Descendant of the sage Bhrigu — a patronymic that became a name in its own right, borne by Shukra and by Parashurama.

The one thing to weigh

It reads faintly Brahmin, since Bhrigu is one of the chief Brahmanical lineages. It is used across communities today and is not a real barrier, but for a Goyal family it is worth naming rather than discovering later. It is also four syllables against Goyal's two, which is a heavier pairing than the three above.

The astrology of the surname

Goyal is a Venus name, and so is he

The alignment

GOYAL totals 15, which reduces to 6 — Venus, Shukra. That is not a neutral fact for this child. Venus is the ruling planet of Purva Ashadha, the nakshatra his Moon was in at birth. And Venus is the lord of his birth mahadasha — he was born into a Venus period with 2 years, 2 months and 15 days still to run. The inherited surname and the sky he was born under are governed by the same planet.

The complication, stated plainly

Venus is a declared enemy of the Sun, and the Sun is his Moolank. So the surname is pulling on a planet that his birth number does not get along with. This is exactly why full-name totals landing on root 6 fail the gate — 42 and 24 are both perfectly fortunate compounds that die on their root. The first name has to carry the correction.

Practically: a first name totalling 21 pushes the pair to 36, root 9, Mars — a friend of the Sun and his own Bhagyank. The Venus weight is answered rather than compounded.

Over to you

Where this leaves the decision

If you want one answer, it is Dhyann

It is the only name that does not require you to pick a school first. Under the first-name reading it is 21, the Crown of the Magi. Under the full-name reading it is 36, filed with the Sceptre. Nothing else on either page does that.

Dhanvin is the real casualty — you should know why

It was second on the first page and it does not survive here. Dhanvin Goyal totals 42, which is a genuinely fortunate compound, but 42 reduces to 6 — Venus — and Venus is a declared enemy of the Sun, his Moolank. It is the same failure as Dhruv: a good compound sitting on a hostile root. If you were leaning toward Dhanvin, that decision now depends entirely on whether you score the first name alone.

Tell your Pandit the F letter came back

He gave you bh, dh and F. I ruled F out on the first page and that was correct for the first name alone. With the surname attached, Phalit Goyal works — 37, root 1, the Sun. If he intended the full name all along, his third letter was never wrong.

First name alone, or the name written on documents. It is the one question that decides between Dhyann, Dheer, Phalit and Dhanvin — and I can finish the remaining checks on whichever you pick.
