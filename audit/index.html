So the second table does not overturn the answer, but it does thin the field behind it.

Cross-check two

The digits his birth date is missing

The gap

Written out, his date of birth is 2 8 0 7 2 0 2 6. The digits it never uses are 1, 3, 4, 5 and 9 — and that list contains something worth pausing on: both his Moolank (1) and his Bhagyank (9) are absent from the date itself. A school of Indian numerology holds that the name should supply what the chart of birth left out.

What each name supplies

Dhyann introduces 1, 3 and 9 — which is to say it supplies both of the missing numbers that govern him, plus Jupiter's 3. Dharmik fills the most ground (1, 3, 4, 9) but fails the full-name gate on other grounds. Dhruv fills only 3 and 5 and misses both of his governing digits, which is a third independent reason it is the wrong name for this child.

Stated plainly: this is a secondary school of thought, not a primary gate. I am reporting it because you asked for every reading, not because it outranks the compound and the root.

Still outstanding

What I have not done, and whether it matters

Shadbala and Ashtakavarga

These are the two real numerical strength systems in Vedic astrology, and I have computed neither. Both are substantial implementations rather than a formula, and I would rather tell you they are missing than estimate them. What they would affect: how confidently anyone can call one planet “strongest” in this chart. I have already pulled that claim off the first page for exactly this reason — it now says only that Mercury sits in its own sign, which is a fact rather than a ranking.

Divisional charts beyond the Navamsa

I computed the D9 and stopped. The D10 (career), D12 (parents) and D60 (fine detail) are not done. What they would affect: nothing about the name. The naming syllable is fixed by the Moon's pada in the rashi chart, and no divisional chart overrides it.

Strictly, no name on any of these pages starts with his exact syllable

This is the sharpest if-and-but in the project and it deserves stating plainly. Pada 4's syllable is ढा — a retroflex ḍha, Unicode U+0922. Every Dh- name recommended here begins with ध, the dental dha, U+0927 — which is pada 2. They collapse to the same “Dha” in Roman script, but they are different Sanskrit consonants. What it would affect: in practice nothing — families and pandits universally treat the roman “Dh” as covering both, and the retroflex ढ is so rare in Sanskrit that it yields essentially no usable modern name. But if your Pandit is strict about the pada rather than the nakshatra, this is the question to put to him, and it applies to every Dh name you will be offered anywhere.

The name he will actually be called

This is the one genuine open question, and it is not a calculation. Cheiro's own rule is that the operative number belongs to the name a person is most known by — which for a newborn is unknowable. If the family settles on a pet name at home, that name carries its own number, and it is the one that will do the work for the first decade. What it would affect: potentially everything, which is why the recommendation is the one name that holds under both the first-name and full-name readings. It is the most robust answer available to a question that will not be fully settled for years.

And the honest structural limit

Everything here is computation and textual sourcing. A practising jyotishi reading this chart as a whole would weigh things a script cannot — the interplay of the yogas, the temperament the combination suggests, judgement built on many charts. This work is meant to give your Pandit something precise to react to, not to replace him.

The bottom line

Nothing here changes the recommendation. Three things reinforce it.

The Navamsa cannot contradict the pada, because pada 4 is the fourth navamsa of that same arc — they are two views of one 3°20' slice of sky. All four doshas are clear. And Dhyann now additionally survives the Pythagorean table intact and supplies both of the digits his birth date is missing. It was the answer on arithmetic; it is still the answer after the audit.
